### Abstract
We introduce a simple scalar quantity measuring the maximal horizontal deviation of the nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function from the critical line. This quantity, denoted as V_RH, is equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis in the sense that it vanishes if and only if all nontrivial zeros lie on the critical line. Our purpose is not to provide new analytic results on the Riemann Hypothesis, but to present this deviation as a convenient parameter for organizing numerical, analytic, and stability-related discussions in a continuous rather than binary framework. We establish fundamental properties of V_RH, its relationship to classical analytic bounds, numerical stability under perturbations, and its application to organizing discussions of Riemann Hypothesis variants.

### Description
This paper introduces a new formalism for studying the Riemann Hypothesis through a scalar deviation measure. Rather than treating RH as a binary statement, we parametrize the degree of deviation from the critical line through the V_RH functional. This framework enables:

1. **Continuous parameterization** of zero distribution properties
2. **Stability analysis** under numerical perturbations
3. **Integration with analytic bounds** and zero-free regions
4. **Unified framework** for discussing RH variants

The work is motivated by the need for a mathematical language that bridges numerical exploration and theoretical rigor.
